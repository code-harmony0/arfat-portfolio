The product

Keys On Rent is a property rental platform. Rather than one app trying to serve everyone, it ships three: one for tenants, one for landlords, and one for the people who run the buildings.

The brief

A rental business wanted tenants, landlords and building staff on their phones. That is three kinds of user doing three different jobs over the same rental data, with a two-month window and two developers.

How I read it

The risk with three apps is that they drift apart. The tenant app and the landlord app show the same rent and the same maintenance ticket from opposite sides. So the work that mattered most was agreeing what a tenancy, a payment and a ticket are, once, and giving each role only the part it needs.

What I built

  • Tenant app: listings, viewings, owner messaging, rent payments and maintenance tickets.
  • Landlord app: a portfolio view across properties.
  • Operations app: building operations, published as KOR Manager on Google Play.

How we ran it

Two developers, two months, three store releases. With that budget each app launched with the core of its own job rather than a little of everything: tenants got listings, viewings, owner messaging, rent payments and maintenance tickets, landlords got a portfolio across properties, and operations got the building tools.

Time, not technology, was the constraint, so every choice was measured against what would be live in both stores in two months.

What I shipped

All three applications on Google Play and the App Store. The tenant app has 1,000+ installs. Links above.
